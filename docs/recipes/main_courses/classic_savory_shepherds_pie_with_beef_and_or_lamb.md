# Classic Savory Shepherd's Pie With Beef And Or Lamb

## Ingredients

### Mashed Potatoes And Meat Sauce

- 3 1/2 pounds (1.6kg) russet potatoes (about 4 large), peeled and cut into 1-inch pieces
- Kosher salt
- 6 tablespoons (85g) unsalted butter, cubed
- 1 1/2 cups (360ml) homemade or store-bought low-sodium chicken stock
- 2 packets unflavored gelatin (about 5 teaspoons; 1/2 ounce; 14g)
- 2 tablespoons (30ml) vegetable oil
- 2 1/2 pounds (1kg) ground beef or lamb, or some combination (see note)
- 1 large yellow onion (about 14 ounces; 400g), diced
- 3 medium carrots (about 8 ounces; 225g), diced
- 2 ribs celery (about 4 ounces; 110g), diced
- 2 medium cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons (30ml) tomato paste
- 1 cup (240ml) dry red wine
- 2 sprigs thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 tablespoon (15ml) Worcestershire sauce
- 1 teaspoon (5ml) Marmite (optional)
- 2 tablespoons (15g) all-purpose flour
- 8 ounces (225g) frozen peas
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 1/2 cups (360ml) heavy cream
- Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, for topping (optional)

## Instructions

1. Rinse the potatoes in a colander under cold water until the water runs clear. Transfer them to a large saucepan, cover with cold water by at least 2 inches, and season the water with salt until almost as salty as the sea.
2. Bring the potatoes to a boil over high heat, then reduce the heat to medium-low and simmer until a knife pierces them easily with no resistance, 10 to 15 minutes.
3. Drain the potatoes, rinse them with hot running water for 30 seconds, and transfer them to a large bowl.
4. Mash the potatoes with the butter using a potato masher, food mill, or ricer. Smooth the surface, press plastic wrap directly against it to prevent a skin from forming, and set aside.
5. Place the stock in a 2-cup liquid measuring cup, sprinkle with gelatin, and set aside.
6. Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over high heat until shimmering. Add half of the ground meat and cook, stirring and scraping the bottom of the pot, until well browned, 6 to 8 minutes. Break up the meat with a potato masher or large whisk.
7. Add the remaining meat and cook, breaking it up, until reduced to small bits, about 3 minutes. Lower the heat as needed to prevent scorching.
8. If the meat has rendered an excessive amount of fat, ladle most of it out with a metal spoon, leaving a few tablespoons in the pot.
9. Add the onion, carrots, celery, and garlic. Cook, stirring and scraping the bottom of the pot, until just beginning to soften, about 4 minutes.
10. Add the tomato paste and cook over medium heat, stirring, for 1 minute.
11. Add the red wine and bring to a simmer over high heat. Cook, scraping up the browned bits, until almost fully evaporated.
12. Add the reserved chicken stock, thyme, bay leaf, Worcestershire sauce, and Marmite, if using.
13. Sprinkle the flour over the meat, then stir it in. Bring the sauce to a simmer, reduce the heat to low, and simmer until reduced and thick, about 20 minutes.
14. Discard the thyme sprigs and bay leaf. Stir in the peas and season with salt and pepper.
15. Adjust the oven rack to the center position and preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C).
16. Heat the cream in a large saucepan until simmering. Add the potatoes and stir gently until completely incorporated. Season with salt and pepper.
17. Set a 9- by 13-inch baking dish on a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet. Add the meat sauce, being careful not to fill the dish more than halfway; you might not need all the sauce, depending on the exact size of the dish.
18. Top with the mashed potatoes and spread them with a spatula to cover the surface completely. Create a dappled pattern on top with the spatula, then sprinkle with grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, if using.
19. Bake until the top is browned and the casserole is heated through, about 20 minutes.
20. For deeper browning, place the casserole on a rack about 6 inches under a hot broiler for the last few moments of cooking. Watch closely to prevent the potatoes from burning.
21. Let the pie stand 15 to 20 minutes before serving.

## Notes

- The meat can be ground beef, lamb, or a combination. Marmite and Parmigiano-Reggiano are optional. Depending on the exact size of the baking dish, you might not need all the sauce.

Classic, Savory Shepherd's Pie (with Beef and/or Lamb)

Ingredients

  • 3 1/2 lb (1.6kg) russet potatoes (about 4 large), peeled and cut into 1-inch pieces
  • kosher salt
  • 6 Tbsp (85g) unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1 1/2 cups (360ml) homemade or store-bought low-sodium chicken stock
  • 2 packets unflavored gelatin (about 5 tsp; 1/2 oz; 14g)
  • 2 Tbsp (30ml) vegetable oil
  • 2 1/2 lb (1kg) ground beef or lamb, or some combination (see note)
  • 1 large yellow onion (about 14 oz; 400g), diced
  • 3 medium carrots (about 8 oz; 225g), diced
  • 2 ribs celery (about 4 oz; 110g), diced
  • 2 medium cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 Tbsp (30ml) tomato paste
  • 1 cup (240ml) dry red wine
  • 2 sprigs thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 Tbsp (15ml) Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tsp (5ml) marmite (optional)
  • 2 Tbsp (15g) all-purpose flour
  • 8 oz (225g) frozen peas
  • kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 1/2 cups (360ml) heavy cream
  • grated parmigiano-reggiano cheese, for topping (optional)

Instructions

  1. Set cubed potatoes in a colander and rinse under cold water until the water runs clear. Transfer to a large saucepan, cover with cold water by at least 2 inches, and season the water with salt until almost as salty as the sea.
  2. Bring the potatoes to a boil over high heat, then reduce the heat to medium-low and simmer until a knife easily pierces them with no resistance, 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. Drain the potatoes in a colander, rinse with hot running water for 30 seconds, and transfer them to a large bowl.
  4. Using a potato masher, food mill, or ricer, mash the potatoes with butter. Press the surface smooth, cover it directly with plastic wrap to prevent a skin from forming, and set aside until ready to assemble.
  5. Place the stock in a 2-cup liquid measuring cup, sprinkle with gelatin, and set aside.
  6. Heat oil in a large Dutch oven over high heat until shimmering. Add half of the ground meat and cook, stirring and scraping the bottom of the pot, until well browned, 6 to 8 minutes. Use a potato masher or large whisk to break up the meat.
  7. Add the remaining meat and cook, breaking it up with the masher or whisk, until reduced to small bits, about 3 minutes. Lower the heat as necessary to prevent scorching.
  8. If the meat has rendered an excessive amount of fat, ladle most of it out with a metal spoon, leaving just a few tablespoons in the pot.
  9. Add the onion, carrots, celery, and garlic. Cook, stirring and scraping the bottom of the pot, until just beginning to soften slightly, about 4 minutes.
  10. Add the tomato paste and cook over medium heat, stirring, for 1 minute.
  11. Add the red wine and bring to a simmer over high heat. Cook, scraping up any browned bits, until almost fully evaporated.
  12. Add the reserved chicken stock, thyme, bay leaf, Worcestershire, and Marmite, if using.
  13. Sprinkle the flour over the ground meat in the pot, then stir it in. Bring to a simmer, reduce the heat to low, and simmer until the sauce is reduced and thick, about 20 minutes.
  14. Discard the thyme sprigs and bay leaf. Stir in the peas and season with salt and pepper.
  15. Adjust the oven rack to the center position and preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C).
  16. Before assembling, heat the cream in a large saucepan until simmering. Add the potatoes and stir gently until completely incorporated. Season with salt and pepper.
  17. Set a 9- by 13-inch baking dish on a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet. Add the meat sauce, being careful not to fill the dish more than halfway. You might not need all of the sauce, depending on the exact size of your baking dish.
  18. Top with the mashed potatoes, spreading them with a spatula to cover the surface completely. Use the spatula to create a dappled pattern on top of the potatoes.
  19. Sprinkle with grated Parmesan cheese, if using.
  20. Transfer the casserole to the oven and bake until the top is browned and the casserole is fully heated through, about 20 minutes.
  21. For deeper browning, place the casserole on a rack set about 6 inches under a hot broiler for the last few moments of cooking. Monitor closely to prevent the potatoes from burning.
  22. Let stand 15 to 20 minutes before serving.
